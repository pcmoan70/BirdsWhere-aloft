Winter irruption outlook 2026/27

Norway & Sweden · issued

How many waxwings, pine grosbeaks, crossbills, siskins, redpolls and other irruptive birds will this winter bring? The bars are the share of birding visits expected to report each species between October and March, region by region, from fifty-six winters of observations (Artsobservasjoner and Artportalen via GBIF) and the seed and berry crops that drive them. The percentage is the chance of an irruption winter — a share above the region's upper third of past winters.

What drives this winter's numbers

With no crop index published yet for the 2026 season anywhere in Fennoscandia, this outlook uses the two things known on the issue date: last winter's share (the carry-over of population and depletion) and the early-autumn share (1 August – 15 September, the first sign of birds on the move — decisive for crossbills and nutcrackers, uninformative for waxwings and pine grosbeaks, which arrive later). The crop indices below enter the hindcasts and show, species by species, how much a crop index would add when one exists for the current year.

How good is it — hindcasts 1995/96 – 2014/15

Every winter in that window was forecast from the winters before it only (rolling origin, whole seasons held out), for all six regions. M0 uses last winter; M0e adds the early-autumn share; M1 adds the Finnish crop index of the same year (the only Nordic series long enough). MAE is the mean absolute error of the log share; Brier scores the irruption-winter call (0 = perfect, 0.25 = coin toss). The crop column is the MAE change M1 − M0e with a 90 % season-block bootstrap interval — negative means the crop index helped.

The crops

Standardised national indices from MASTREE+ (Hacket-Pain et al. 2022, CC BY 4.0): Finland's rowan berry index 1986–2014, spruce cone index 1979–2014, birch seed index 1979–2014 (mean of silver and downy birch). Nordic masting is broadly synchronous, so the Finnish series serves as the regional crop signal for Norway and Sweden too — and, by the same token, cannot tell local from source-area food. The Norwegian Skogfrøverket cone grades (0–4) exist for 2023–2025 only: spruce 0.9 → 2.2 → 0.3, pine 1.4 → 1.7 → 1.2 (national means, lowland band); too short to model, listed here for the record.

What this cannot say

  • Observations are opportunistic: the share per visit corrects for how much people go birding, not for where they go, how long they stay, or that a flock of waxwings in a town gets reported more than one in a forest.
  • Six coarse regions, one national crop series: the model knows nothing about a given valley, coast or garden.
  • No crop index exists for this year, so this winter's outlook is the baseline model (last winter + early autumn). The hindcast table says what a crop index would add; the Finnish series stop in 2014 and the Norwegian cone grades begin in 2023.
  • Finland's birds are not in the model yet (no long observation archive on hand); the Finnish crop stands in only as a regional food signal.
  • A skilful hindcast is not a mechanism: the positive crop effect for waxwing and pine grosbeak is consistent with birds being pulled to berry-rich regions in good rowan years, as Dale (2023) found, but push, pull and population size are not separated here.

Sources & method

Bird observations: GBIF occurrence downloads of all birds in Norway and Sweden 1970–2026 (doi:10.15468/dl.h9jvy6 and its 1970–2009 sibling), which carry the Artsobservasjoner and Artportalen records; last observation used: . Crops: MASTREE+ v2 (CC BY 4.0); Skogfrøverket seed-setting reports 2023–2025. Models: ordinary least squares on the log share with a region intercept; the irruption probability is the Gaussian tail above the region's 67th percentile of past winters, with the training residual sd. Literature: Dale 2023 (J. Ornithol. 164: 353, 887), Dale & Edvardsen 2024 (165: 897), Dale, Ekebom & Dahl 2025 (Ornis Svecica 35).
